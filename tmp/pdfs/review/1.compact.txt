Introduction The Philippines is the main exporter of bananas in Asia on the global market which supplies some 60 percent of Asian banana shipments on average, as reported from the Banana Market Review of Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, FAO (2023). It is noted that more and more consumers prefer to buy bananas exported from the Philippines. In the Banana Industry Profile by Bautista (2021), three major varieties are stated to be produced in the country, ranking Cavendish as the primary variety with 50 percent of the total banana production, Lakatan as the second, and Saba as the third variety. Banana is one of the most important fruit crops in the Philippines and it is commonly exported fresh and processed like banana chips. However, in 2024, shipments dropped by 3 percent due to inclement weather and a plant disease outbreak causing the Philippines to slip to 4th in banana export ranking (Lagare, 2025). Production losses, tariff and logistical difficulties recently caused the decline in banana exportation and it is only recently that the Philippines have regained its second spot as top exporter in Asia. The Department of Trade and Industry DTI (2026) said that reclaiming the spot has a significant economic impact in the Philippines as bananas remain as the major source of rural employment and foreign exchange earnings. Dhareshwar (2022) reported in an article that CALABARZON ranked as the 11th top producer on the annual production of banana in 2021 with over 26,993 hectares cultivated that yields 3,384 kgs per hectare. Project Context Many farmers rely on banana cultivation as a source of income and food supply in the tropical regions including the Philippines. However, there are threats in banana production due to various diseases that affect its growth and overall health. Diseases such as leaf spots and other fungal infections often appear first on banana leaves that eventually reduce the plant's capacity to photosynthesize thus lowering yield and fruit quality. It is essential to have early detection of these diseases for effective disease management, yet there are farmers relying on manual inspection that can be time-consuming and prone to misidentification. Moreover, plant disease diagnostic laboratories with agricultural experts aren't accessible in many farming areas making it difficult for farmers to determine disease symptoms during its early stage. Traditional detection methods require laboratory procedures and other specialized equipment which are not practical and accessible especially for small-scale farmers. As a result, disease outbreaks may spread before proper control measures are implemented leading to crop damage and reduced productivity. The lack of accessible and fast diagnostic tools highlights the need for a more efficient and farmer-friendly approach in detecting banana diseases. The researchers propose developing BananaQ, a mobile application to detect and classify banana leaf diseases. The system will help analyze images of banana leaves and automatically identify potential diseases. Through this approach, the

Purpose and Description Banana is one of the most important agricultural crops in the Philippines, serving as a major source of food, livelihood, and income for many farming communities. The country is recognized as one of the world's leading producers and exporters of bananas, making the industry a significant contributor to the agricultural sector and the national economy. Aside from its economic value, bananas are widely consumed by Filipinos and are used in various food products, highlighting their importance in ensuring food security and supporting local livelihoods. Despite the significance of banana production, farmers continue to face numerous challenges that affect crop yield and quality. Among these challenges are plant diseases that commonly attack banana leaves and reduce the productivity of banana plantations. Diseases such as Black Sigatoka, Panama Disease, and Cordana Leaf Spot can cause severe damage to banana plants when not detected and treated at an early stage. These diseases often manifest through visible symptoms on banana leaves, including discoloration, lesions, and abnormal spotting, which may spread rapidly and result in substantial crop losses. Furthermore, many farmers have limited access to agricultural specialists and diagnostic services, making timely disease identification difficult. The increasing demand for sustainable agricultural practices and improved crop management highlights the need for accessible technological solutions that can assist farmers in monitoring plant health. Early detection and proper disease management are essential in minimizing crop damage, reducing production losses, and maintaining the quality of banana harvests. The use of mobile technology presents an opportunity to provide farmers with convenient access to disease

Scope and Limitation of the Study This study focuses on the development of BananaQ, a mobile application designed to detect diseases in banana leaves. The system analyzes images of banana leaves to classify the type of disease and provide early detection recommendations to farmers in Barangay San Felix, Sto. Tomas City, Batangas. It aims to assist farmers in improving crop health management, reducing losses, and promoting timely disease detection. The system will not address other environmental or agricultural factors such as soil fertility, irrigation, pest infestation, or fertilizer application. The study will be limited exclusively to a banana farm in Barangay San Felix, Sto. Tomas City, Batangas. The study will be conducted from the Summer Term of Academic Year 2025–2026 through the First Semester of Academic Year 2026–2027. Furthermore, the researchers acknowledge the following limitations: ● The software focused solely on the farmers of Barangay San Felix, Sto. Tomas City, Batangas; therefore, its findings may not be applicable to other nearby barangays, cities, or municipalities. ● The application focuses only on banana leaf disease detection, such as Black Sigatoka, Panama Disease, and Cordana Leaf Spot. Newly discovered or uncommon diseases may not be recognized and do not support the detection of diseases from other crops or plants. ● The application will identify the presence and type of disease only and will not determine the severity or stage of infection. Therefore, it will not provide assessments regarding whether the disease is in its early, moderate, or severe stage.

Theoretical Framework The research will be anchored in the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) proposed by Davis (1989) and the ISO/IEC 25010 standard established by the International Organization for Standardization (2011), both of which will provide foundational frameworks for evaluating user acceptance and software quality, respectively. The theoretical framework for the implementation of BananaQ: A Mobile Application for Banana Leaf Disease Detection with Treatment and Prevention Recommendations will employ the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) to understand how farmers and potential users will accept and use mobile technology for agricultural disease detection. Developed by Davis (1989), TAM explains that users' acceptance of a technology will primarily be influenced by their perceptions of its usefulness and ease of use.

13 Conceptual Framework Figure 3. Input Process Output (IPO) Diagram The conceptual framework of this study is anchored on the Input-Process-Output (IPO) model, which serves as a guide in the development and evaluation of the proposed BananaQ mobile application. The input consists of banana leaf disease images, disease-related information from credible sources, and expert knowledge. These inputs provide the essential resources needed to develop a reliable system that can assist in the early detection and management of banana leaf diseases. The process begins with data gathering, where images of both healthy and diseased banana leaves are collected along with relevant information on disease symptoms, treatment options, and preventive measures. User requirements are also gathered to gain a deeper understanding of the challenges and needs of farmers and other potential users. By listening to their experiences and concerns, the researchers can ensure that the application is designed to address real-world problems encountered in banana farming.
=== PDF PAGE 14 ===
14 After the necessary data has been collected, the study proceeds to data integration and preparation. During this stage, the images are organized, labeled, cleaned, and preprocessed to improve their quality and suitability for model training. At the same time, disease-related information is integrated into the system to ensure that users receive accurate and helpful recommendations whenever a disease is identified. The next stage involves model development and analysis. A deep learning model based on MobileNetV3 is trained and tested using the prepared dataset to enable the system to recognize and classify different banana leaf diseases. Once the model has been developed, it is integrated into the BananaQ application together with a user-friendly interface that allows users to easily capture images, view disease detection results, and access corresponding treatment and prevention recommendations. To ensure that the application performs effectively and meets user expectations, it is evaluated using the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standards and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). These evaluations help determine the system's quality, usability, reliability, and overall acceptance among its intended users. The output of the study is a mobile application capable of detecting and classifying banana leaf diseases while providing practical information that can guide users in managing affected crops. More importantly, the application aims to empower farmers by providing timely and reliable disease detection, helping them make informed decisions, reduce potential crop losses, and promote healthier and more productive banana plantations. Through this technology, the study hopes to contribute to improved agricultural practices and support the livelihoods of individuals and communities that depend on banana farming.
