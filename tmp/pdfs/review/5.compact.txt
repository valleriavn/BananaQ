Tomas Campus (5) Background and Significance Diseases such as leaf spots and other fungal infections often appear first on banana leaves. It is essential to have early detection of these diseases for effective disease management, yet there are farmers relying on manual inspection that can be time consuming and prone to misidentification. To address this issue, the study proposes a mobile application for detecting and classifying banana leaf diseases called BananaQ. The app will be tested by banana farmers in Barangay San Felix, Sto. Tomas City, Batangas. The expected output will benefit banana farmers in early detection of banana leaf diseases by ensuring a reliable prediction of disease in bananas. Study Design ● Quantitative Research Design ● Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) ● Survey Questionnaire ● Purposive Sampling B. Study Population ● The study will involve a total of twelve (12) participants, consisting of two (2) Agricultural IT Experts and ten (10) banana farmers. Assessment of Resources ● The study has sufficient access to the study population through established coordination with San Felix Coop of banana farmers and barangay officials of San Felix. The researchers have allotted enough time within the academic calendar to conduct recruitment, data collection, analysis, and reporting. The research team consists of qualified student investigators supervised by a faculty adviser, ensuring methodological rigor and adherence to ethical standards. All members of the research team have been oriented on the study protocol, ethical considerations, and their research-related duties. D. Study Procedures This study aims to evaluate the BananaQ mobile application in improving the efficiency and accuracy of banana leaf disease detection and classification among banana farmers in Barangay San Felix, Sto. Tomas, Batangas. The research focuses on assessing the app’s usability, reliability, and overall effectiveness by gathering feedback from banana farmers who are actively involved in banana cultivation and are familiar with common banana leaf diseases affecting crop productivity. Participants will be given approximately 15-20 minutes to interact with the application on provided devices. The study procedure includes the following steps: ● Development of the Survey Questionnaire. The questionnaire will be developed by the researchers and validated through the appropriate research and ethics review body of the Polytechnic University of the Philippines (PUP). Its content aims to measure how practical the app is, how simple it is to operate, and whether participants plan to use it in the future. In addition, the study utilizes international standards for software quality and systematically gathers participant feedback. This framework ensures the application is evaluated not only for its practical utility but also for its technical robustness specifically assessing its functional correctness, operational reliability, user accessibility, performance efficiency, data security, and cross-platform compatibility. The instrument will use a five-point Likert scale with appropriate response options for each evaluation criterion. An informed consent form will also be included at the beginning of the questionnaire to ensure that participants understand their voluntary participation and rights. ● Participant Recruitment and Application Trial. A formal coordination request will be made with the barangay officials of San Felix, Sto. Tomas, Batangas, to secure permission for the involvement of banana farmers. With respect to the Agricultural IT experts, the researchers will coordinate with the local Department of Agriculture in Sto. Tomas to identify qualified professionals who can validate the system. Participants will be selected through purposive sampling, focusing on individuals who are actively engaged in banana farming and have basic familiarity with mobile technology. A total of twelve (12) participants, consisting of two (2) Agricultural IT Experts and ten (10) banana farmers. Participants will evaluate the BananaQ mobile application by testing its key features: image-based banana leaf disease detection, disease classification, and treatment and prevention recommendations. This evaluation will assess the application's accuracy, usability, and effectiveness.

After testing the BananaQ application, participants will accomplish a printed survey questionnaire. The evaluation relies on a questionnaire structured around user acceptance metrics and software quality standards to capture user feedback, perception, the usability of the app, and its overall effectiveness. E. Data Collection ● Feedback Questionnaire Development . A structured questionnaire will be developed to gather quantitative data on user satisfaction, perceived usefulness, ease of use, and overall system performance of the BananaQ mobile application. The questionnaire will be anchored on the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model that gives feedback on core operational traits, including the accuracy of the disease detection features, system stability against crashes, interface clarity, loading speed, data security, and the app's capability to run smoothly across various smartphone models. and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) to measure how practical the banana farmers find the tool in managing crops, as well as how simple and intuitive the interface is to navigate. ● Distribution . The questionnaire will be administered through a face-to-face survey using printed copies only. A total of twelve (12) participants, consisting of two (2) Agricultural IT Experts and ten (10) banana farmers will participate in the study. Assistance will be provided when needed to clarify any items in the questionnaire. After completion, all questionnaires will be collected immediately to ensure completeness and accuracy of the responses. ● Informed Consent . At the beginning of the questionnaire, participants will be provided with a clear explanation of the study’s purpose, procedures, voluntary nature, and data confidentiality measures. Participants will be required to provide consent before proceeding with the survey. F. Data Analysis ● Quantitative Analysis. Descriptive statistics will be applied to summarize user feedback and evaluate the BananaQ mobile application’s usability, usefulness, reliability, and overall effectiveness in detecting and classifying banana leaf diseases. Inferential statistical methods will also be utilized to determine the relationship between how helpful the farmers find the app, how easy it is for them to navigate, and their overall willingness to adopt the technology based on user acceptance metrics from the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). In addition, the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality standards will be used to assess its functional accuracy, resistance to crashes, visual clarity, processing speed, data security, and its ability to run smoothly across different smartphone models. The results of both user acceptance metrics and software standard quality evaluations will provide a comprehensive analysis of the application’s performance and quality. ● Purposive Analysis. Purposive sampling will be employed to ensure that the respondents, particularly banana farmers from San Felix, Sto. Tomas City, Batangas, have direct experience in banana farming and are familiar with the common banana leaf diseases that the BananaQ mobile application aims to detect and classify. This approach ensures that the collected responses are relevant and aligned with the objectives of the study. (8) Safety and Monitoring Plan

This ensures that the data becomes irretrievable and cannot be accessed or misused in any form. Proper disposal procedures will be followed to maintain confidentiality and comply with ethical data management standards. (9) Limitations The researchers acknowledge that certain limitations in the study’s design, methodology, and technological requirements may influence the interpretation of the findings: 1. Geographic and Demographic Scope. The software focused solely on the farmers of Barangay San Felix, Sto. Tomas City, Batangas; its findings may not be applicable to other nearby barangays, cities, or municipalities. 2. Limited Disease Coverage. The application focuses only on banana leaf disease detection, such as black sigatoka, panama disease, and cordana leaf spot. Newly discovered or uncommon diseases may not be recognized, and the system does not support the detection of diseases from other crops or plants. 3. Internet Connectivity Dependence. Users are required to have an active internet connection for image submission and system processing. Limited or unstable mobile data access in some areas may affect usability. 4. Platform Limitation. The application was developed exclusively for Android users; iOS users were not supported. This restricts the accessibility of the system to a single mobile operating platform. 5. Image Quality Constraints. The accuracy of the system depends on the quality of the captured banana leaf image; unclear or poorly lit images may affect the detection results. Environmental factors such as lighting conditions and camera specifications may influence system performance. (10) Ethical Considerations

Informed Consent (Applies to studies using human subjects) All participants will be informed about the purpose, scope, and procedures of the study before their participation. Participants may withdraw at any time before data analysis; their responses will be discarded upon request. The researchers will obtain consent through a printed informed consent form attached to the survey questionnaire. Only those who agree after reading and understanding the information provided will be allowed to participate in the study. The consent form will clearly state that participation is voluntary and that participants may withdraw from the study at any time without any penalty or consequences. B. Risks and Side Effects (Applies to studies using human subjects) Participation in the study poses minimal to no risk. The primary inconvenience is the time commitment required to answer the survey questionnaire, which will take approximately 20–30 minutes. Participants may also experience digital discomfort, such as frustration when using the application or encountering minor technical issues or app glitches. Assistance will be provided to participants who experience difficulty using the application. Participants may use their own mobile data during the app trial, which may incur minimal costs. There is also a potential risk of misinterpretation or reliance on the application's disease detection results and recommendations. The application provides recommendations based on image analysis, but these do not replace the advice of a licensed agricultural expert. Farmers are encouraged to consult agricultural experts for confirmation before making decisions based on the application's results. To mitigate these risks, the researchers will be present during the app trial to provide assistance, answer questions, address technical concerns, and clarify that the application's recommendations are advisory only. No medical, psychological, legal, financial, or significant social risks are expected. C. Benefits to Subjects (Applies to studies using human subjects) The study benefits participants by providing them with improved support for banana disease detection and crop management through the development of the BananaQ application. Additionally, their feedback will directly inform improvements to the system, ensuring that it better addresses the real-world needs of banana farmers in identifying and managing banana diseases. On a broader scale, the study contributes to enhancing agricultural productivity and supporting farmers in making more informed decisions regarding banana crop health. D. Costs to Subject (Applies to studies using human subjects) No fees or costs required for participation, as all activities will be conducted face-to-face using printed materials. Participants will only need to cover for the possible use of mobile data or internet connection when accessing and testing the BananaQ application E. Compensation to Subject (Applies to studies using human subjects) No monetary payment or material compensation will be provided, as participation in the study is entirely Voluntary. F. Provisions for vulnerable subjects (Applies to studies using human subjects) No other vulnerable groups, such as PWDs or indigenous peoples, are expected to be specifically recruited.

Subject Privacy and Data Confidentiality (Applies to studies using human subjects) All data and information collected from the participants will be kept strictly confidential and used solely for the purposes of this study. The data collected will be limited to the participants’ age and survey responses only. No names or other personal identifiers will be collected. To ensure anonymity, each participant’s responses will be assigned a code (e.g., Participant 1, Participant 2) instead of using identifying information. The completed printed questionnaires will be securely stored in a locked cabinet within the researchers’ designated workspace. Encoded electronic data will be stored in a password-protected folder on a secured, offline computer to prevent unauthorized access. Access to the collected data will be strictly limited to the five (5) researchers and the research adviser. Participants have the right to withdraw from the study at any time. If a participant chooses to withdraw, their collected data will be removed and excluded from the analysis, subject to the point at which the data have already been anonymized or incorporated into aggregated results. All research data will be retained for one (1) year after the completion of the study. After the retention period, all physical questionnaires will be securely shredded, while electronic files will be permanently deleted from the secured computer and any designated storage locations. These measures will be implemented to ensure the continued confidentiality, privacy, and protection of the participants’ information. (11) Plan for Dissemination of Findings The findings of this capstone project will be disseminated through a formal defense presentation, submission of the research paper, and potential publication or presentation in academic forums.

Mesa, Manila 1016 Trunk Line: 335-1787 or 335-1777 Website: www.pup.edu.ph | Inquiries: https://bit.ly/PUPSINTA A L EADING C OMPREHENSIVE P OLYTECHNIC U NIVERSITY IN A SIA The research instruments consist of a self-developed printed survey questionnaire and a consent form. The survey is designed to gather quantitative feedback from banana farmers regarding the performance of the BananaQ mobile application. It evaluates the system based on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) to measure user acceptance, focusing on perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, behavioral intention to use, and overall satisfaction. It also incorporates the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality model to assess the application’s technical quality in terms of functionality, reliability, usability, performance efficiency, compatibility, security, maintainability, and portability. Evaluation of the application is conducted using a 5-point Likert scale as the primary rating instrument. The scale ranges from 5 to 1, where 5 indicates Strongly Agree, 4 Agree, 3 Neutral, 2 Disagree, and 1 Strongly Disagree. This provides a structured method for participants to express their level of agreement with each evaluation criterion and allows the researchers to quantify user perceptions of the system. An informed consent form is provided to all participants before the start of data collection. It clearly explains the purpose of the study, the procedures involved, and the measures in place to ensure confidentiality and data protection. Participation is entirely voluntary, and respondents may withdraw from the study at any time without any consequences. All signed consent forms and completed questionnaires are securely stored by the researchers and accessed only by the research team and authorized faculty advisers.
