Purpose of the Study This study aims to develop and evaluate BananaQ, a mobile application designed to help banana farmers identify diseases affecting banana leaves so they can apply appropriate treatment at an early stage. BananaQ analyzes images of banana leaves and detects common diseases such as Black Sigatoka, Panama Disease, and Cordana Leaf Spot. The application also provides information about the symptoms of each disease, along with simple recommendations for treatment and prevention. Through surveys and feedback from banana farmers, the study will assess the app’s usability, reliability, and overall effectiveness. The findings will help determine how well the application supports farmers in improving disease detection and management for banana crops. The data gathered from this study will be used as part of the researchers’ thesis requirement at the Polytechnic University of the Philippines (PUP). The researchers declare that there are no conflicts of interest related to this project. Procedures of the Study This study aims to evaluate the BananaQ mobile application in improving the efficiency and accuracy of banana leaf disease detection and classification among banana farmers in Barangay San Felix, Sto. Tomas, Batangas. The research focuses on assessing the app’s usability, reliability, and overall effectiveness by gathering feedback from banana farmers who are actively involved in banana cultivation and are familiar with common banana leaf diseases affecting crop productivity. The entire session, including app testing and survey completion, will take approximately 20–30 minutes. The study procedure includes the following steps: ● Development of the Survey Questionnaire. The questionnaire will be developed by the researchers and validated through the appropriate research and ethics review body of the Polytechnic University of the Philippines (PUP). Benefits The study is expected to benefit banana farmers by providing them with a more efficient and accessible way of detecting banana leaf diseases through BananaQ mobile application. By using the system, farmers can better understand the conditions of their crops and detect disease early, allowing them to implement timely treatment and prevention measures. On a broader scale, the study will contribute to the advancement of digital agriculture by integrating artificial intelligence into crop disease detection and management. The results of this study may also serve as a reference for future research and development of similar mobile applications aimed at supporting farmers and improving agricultural practices, thereby promoting innovation in agriculture, information technology, and sustainable farming systems. There is also a potential risk of misinterpretation or over-reliance on the application's disease detection results and treatment recommendations. The application provides recommendations based on image analysis, but these are for informational and advisory purposes only. They are not a substitute for the professional advice of a licensed agricultural expert, agricultural extension worker, or plant pathologist. Farmers are encouraged to consult experts for confirmation before making decisions based on the application's results. To mitigate these risks, the researchers will be present during the app trial to provide assistance, answer questions, address technical concerns, and clarify that the application's recommendations are advisory only. Clear and simple instructions will be provided, and participants will be given sufficient time to complete each activity without pressure.
